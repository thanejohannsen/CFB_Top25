    Contradictions

    When A beats B, B beats C and C beats A, no ordering can satisfy all three. These are the loops in this week's results and which result was set aside in each.

    Results the ranking goes against

    Every head-to-head result this ranking contradicts, least convincing first. A win counts for less when it was narrow, at home, or against the power ratings — and moving a team far from its resume position costs more than moving it a little. Overriding any result costs something real: the cheapest one on the board still prices at a full unit of conviction, so a result is only set aside when the rest of the evidence genuinely outweighs it.

    Upset regressions

    When a result spans a wide gap, both teams are pulled toward their midpoint: the upset says the winner was underrated and the loser overrated.
