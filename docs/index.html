A Top 25 built from three numbers, then reordered so it contradicts as few head-to-head results as possible. Every placement opens to show its arithmetic.

How the ranking works

Four stages. The first three produce a score; the fourth reorders it so the ranking contradicts as few head-to-head results as possible.

Stage 1 — the three numbers

Every team gets three national ranks, and lower is better:

  • SoR — the résumé. Who you beat and how hard that was. We walk a top-25 team through your exact schedule and ask how often it would finish with your record. Rarely means a better résumé.
  • Mkt — Vegas's power ranking. How good the money thinks you are, in points on a neutral field. Solved from betting lines, so +20 beats +15 by five on a neutral site.
  • PPA — the game tape, counted. How well you actually played, snap by snap: points gained per play on offence less points allowed on defence, adjusted for who you played.

Mkt is an opinion formed before games; PPA measures what happened during them. That is why both are here. The résumé carries the most weight, because what a team has achieved should outrank how good it looks. Combined they give the Base column.

Stage 2 — the résumé adjustment

A record alone does not say how you got it, so four things move the Base score: how your losses look (a one-point defeat on the road is not a blowout at home), your best win, how comfortably you win, and how you played against the number — beating a bad team by 17 when you were favoured by 28 is a miss, and beating a good one by 1 when you were favoured by 3 is par.

Stage 3 — upset regression

When a result spans a wide gap — the 38th-best résumé beats the 5th — it says both teams were mis-rated, not just one. So both are pulled toward their midpoint. The winner is not teleported to the top and the loser does not get to pretend the game never happened.

Stage 4 — head-to-head

If a team beat you, it is ranked above you. That is a requirement, not a preference, and there are exactly four exceptions:

  • Rule 1 — it has more losses than you. Any gap.
  • Rule 2 — it has lost since the game and you have not. Any gap.
  • Rule 3 — it has lost more often since the game than you have. Any gap.
  • Rule 4 — you have been beating better teams than it has, and at least two weeks have passed. Capped, and the cap grows the longer it stays true.

Nothing else counts. Rules 1 to 3 let the résumé decide how far apart you finish; rule 4 caps it. When the requirement forces a change, both teams move toward each other rather than one being dragged to the other.

Results it still contradicts are listed on the page, and every team’s own card names the rule by number, because “my team beat them and is ranked below them” deserves a real answer.
